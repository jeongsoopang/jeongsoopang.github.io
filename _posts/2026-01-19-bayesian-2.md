## 1) Overview

This section reviews **essential probability distributions**  
and introduces **Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)** as a foundation  
for statistical inference and Bayesian modeling.

---

## 2) Common Probability Distributions

---

## 2.1 Discrete Distributions

### Bernoulli $(p)$

Binary outcome: success (1) or failure (0)

$$
f(x \mid p) = p^x (1 - p)^{1-x}, \quad x \in \{0,1\}
$$

$$
E[X] = p, \quad \mathrm{Var}(X) = p(1 - p)
$$

---

### Binomial $(n, p)$

Number of successes in $n$ independent trials

$$
P(X = x) = \binom{n}{x} p^x (1 - p)^{n-x}
$$

$$
E[X] = np, \quad \mathrm{Var}(X) = np(1 - p)
$$

---

### Negative Binomial $(r, p)$

Number of trials until the $r$-th success

$$
P(X = x) = \binom{x-1}{r-1} p^r (1 - p)^{x-r}
$$

$$
E[X] = \frac{r}{p}, \quad \mathrm{Var}(X) = \frac{r(1 - p)}{p^2}
$$

---

### Geometric $(p)$

Number of trials until the **first** success

$$
P(X = x) = (1 - p)^{x-1} p, \quad x = 1,2,\dots
$$

$$
E[X] = \frac{1}{p}, \quad \mathrm{Var}(X) = \frac{1 - p}{p^2}
$$

---

### Poisson $(\lambda)$

Counts events occurring in a fixed interval

$$
f(x \mid \lambda) = \frac{\lambda^x e^{-\lambda}}{x!},
\quad x = 0,1,2,\dots
$$

$$
E[X] = \mathrm{Var}(X) = \lambda
$$

---

## 2.2 Continuous Distributions

### Uniform $(\theta_1, \theta_2)$

$$
f(x) = \frac{1}{\theta_2 - \theta_1},
\quad x \in [\theta_1, \theta_2]
$$

$$
E[X] = \frac{\theta_1 + \theta_2}{2}, \quad
\mathrm{Var}(X) = \frac{(\theta_2 - \theta_1)^2}{12}
$$

---

### Normal $(\mu, \sigma^2)$

$$
f(x \mid \mu, \sigma) =
\frac{1}{\sigma \sqrt{2\pi}}
\exp\!\left(
-\frac{(x - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}
\right)
$$

$$
E[X] = \mu, \quad \mathrm{Var}(X) = \sigma^2
$$

---

### Exponential $(\lambda)$

$$
f(x \mid \lambda) = \lambda e^{-\lambda x}, \quad x \ge 0
$$

$$
E[X] = \frac{1}{\lambda}, \quad \mathrm{Var}(X) = \frac{1}{\lambda^2}
$$

---

### Gamma $(\alpha, \beta)$

$$
f(x \mid \alpha, \beta) =
\frac{\beta^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)}
x^{\alpha - 1} e^{-\beta x}, \quad x \ge 0
$$

$$
E[X] = \frac{\alpha}{\beta}, \quad
\mathrm{Var}(X) = \frac{\alpha}{\beta^2}
$$

---

### Beta $(\alpha, \beta)$

$$
f(x \mid \alpha, \beta) =
\frac{1}{B(\alpha, \beta)}
x^{\alpha - 1}(1 - x)^{\beta - 1},
\quad 0 \le x \le 1
$$

$$
E[X] = \frac{\alpha}{\alpha + \beta}, \quad
\mathrm{Var}(X) =
\frac{\alpha \beta}{(\alpha + \beta)^2 (\alpha + \beta + 1)}
$$

---

## 3) Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)

### Goal

Estimate parameter $\theta$ that makes the **observed data most probable**.

---

### Likelihood Function

$$
L(\theta; x) = f(x \mid \theta)
$$

For independent samples:

$$
\ell(\theta)
= \ln L(\theta)
= \sum_i \ln f(x_i \mid \theta)
$$

---

### MLE Definition

$$
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{MLE}}
= \arg\max_\theta \ell(\theta)
$$

---

### Example: Coin Flips

Let $x$ heads out of $n$ flips.

$$
L(p; x) = \binom{n}{x} p^x (1 - p)^{n-x}
$$

$$
\ell(p) = x \ln p + (n - x) \ln(1 - p)
$$

$$
\frac{d\ell}{dp} = 0
\quad \Rightarrow \quad
\hat{p}_{\mathrm{MLE}} = \frac{x}{n}
$$

---

### Interpretation

- MLE depends **only on data**
- No prior beliefs involved
- As sample size grows, MLE is consistent

---

## 4) Transition to Bayesian Inference

Frequentist view:
- $\theta$ is **fixed but unknown**

Bayesian view:
- $\theta$ is **random** with prior distribution $p(\theta)$

$$
p(\theta \mid x)
=
\frac{p(x \mid \theta)p(\theta)}{p(x)}
\propto
p(x \mid \theta)p(\theta)
$$

### Key Connection

- **Likelihood** is shared by both frameworks
- Bayesian inference = likelihood × prior

> This naturally leads to the next topic:  
> **Priors and Conjugate Distributions**

---

## Key Takeaway

- Probability distributions define **data models**
- MLE provides a **baseline estimator**
- Bayesian inference generalizes MLE by incorporating prior knowledge
