## 1) Overview

**Bayesian inference** updates belief about unknown parameters using prior knowledge and observed data.

**Goal: compute the posterior distribution**

$$
p(\theta \mid D)
=
\frac{p(D \mid \theta)\,\pi(\theta)}{p(D)}
$$

- Prior $\pi(\theta)$ reflects belief **before** data
- Posterior $p(\theta \mid D)$ reflects belief **after** observing data

The posterior enables:

- Point estimation (MAP, posterior mean)
- Uncertainty quantification (credible intervals)
- Prediction of future data

---

## 2) Improper & Jeffreys’ Priors

| Concept | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Improper Prior** | Prior that does not integrate to 1, but may still yield a proper posterior | $\pi(\sigma^2)\propto \frac{1}{\sigma^2}$ |
| **Jeffreys’ Prior** | Objective noninformative prior derived from Fisher information | $\pi(\theta)\propto \sqrt{I(\theta)}$ |

> An improper prior is acceptable **only if** the resulting posterior is proper.

---

## 3) Posterior Derivation Example  
### Normal Variance with Improper Prior

Let $Y_i \sim \mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2)$ with improper prior:

$$
\pi(\sigma^2)\propto \frac{1}{\sigma^2}
$$

The posterior distribution is:

$$
p(\sigma^2 \mid y)
\propto
(\sigma^2)^{-\frac{n}{2}-1}
\exp\!\left(
-\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}
\sum_{i=1}^n (y_i-\mu)^2
\right)
$$

Hence,

$$
\sigma^2 \mid y
\sim
\mathrm{Inv\text{-}Gamma}\!\left(
\frac{n}{2},\;
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^n (y_i-\mu)^2
\right)
$$

This posterior is **proper**, so the improper prior is valid here.

## 4) Maximum a Posteriori (MAP) Estimation

**MAP estimator = posterior mode**

$$
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{MAP}}
=
\arg\max_\theta p(\theta \mid D)
=
\arg\max_\theta
\big[
\log p(D \mid \theta)
+
\log \pi(\theta)
\big]
$$

### Comparison with MLE

| Estimator | Objective | Prior |
|---|---|---|
| **MLE** | $\arg\max_\theta p(D \mid \theta)$ | None |
| **MAP** | $\arg\max_\theta p(D \mid \theta)\pi(\theta)$ | Included |

---

### 4.1 Example: Bernoulli Data

Data: $x=(0,0,1,1,0)$ from $\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\theta)$

- **MLE**

$$
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{MLE}}=\frac{2}{5}=0.4
$$

- **Discrete prior**

$$
\theta\in\{0.2,0.5,0.7\},
\qquad
\pi=\{0.1,0.01,0.89\}
$$

→ **MAP estimate** is $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{MAP}}=0.7$

## 5) Posterior Mean & Shrinkage

The **posterior mean** balances data evidence with prior belief.

### 5.1 Beta–Binomial Example

If $X_i \sim \mathrm{Bern}(\theta)$ and $\theta \sim \mathrm{Beta}(\alpha,\beta)$:

- **Posterior**

$$
\theta \mid X
\sim
\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha+S_n,\;\beta+n-S_n)
$$

- **Posterior Mean**

$$
\hat{\theta}_E
=
\frac{S_n+\alpha}{n+\alpha+\beta}
=
\lambda\,\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{MLE}}
+
(1-\lambda)\,\theta_{\mathrm{prior}},
\qquad
\lambda=\frac{n}{n+\alpha+\beta}
$$

This illustrates **shrinkage** toward the prior mean when sample size is small.

---

## 6) Posterior Variance

For the Beta–Binomial model:

$$
\mathrm{Var}(\theta \mid X)
=
\frac{(\alpha+S_n)(\beta+n-S_n)}
{(n+\alpha+\beta)^2\,(n+\alpha+\beta+1)}
$$

- As $n$ increases → variance decreases  
- Posterior concentrates around the true parameter value

## 7) Credible Intervals vs Confidence Intervals

| Concept | Frequentist CI | Bayesian Credible Interval |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | $P_\theta(\theta\in C(D))=1-\alpha$ | $P(\theta\in I\mid D)=1-\alpha$ |
| Probability refers to | Data variability | Parameter uncertainty |
| Interpretation | Long-run coverage | Direct probability about $\theta$ |

---

### 7.1 Types of Credible Intervals

- **Equal-tailed interval**: $\alpha/2$ probability in each tail
- **HPD interval**: shortest interval with probability $1-\alpha$
- **One-sided intervals**

---

## 8) Bayesian Prediction

After observing data $D$, the predictive distribution for a future observation $X_{n+1}$ is:

$$
p(x_{n+1} \mid D)
=
\int p(x_{n+1} \mid \theta)\,p(\theta \mid D)\,d\theta
$$

Interpretation:
- A weighted average of likelihoods
- Weights given by posterior uncertainty in $\theta$

---

## Key Takeaway

> Bayesian inference produces a **full posterior distribution**, not just a point estimate.  
> This enables principled estimation, uncertainty quantification, and prediction in a single coherent framework.
