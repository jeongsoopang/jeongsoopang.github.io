## 1) Overview

**Bayesian Decision Theory** provides a principled framework for making optimal decisions under uncertainty by combining:

- **Probability** (Bayesian inference)  
- **Consequences** (loss functions)

**Key idea:**  
Choose the decision that **minimizes expected loss** under the posterior distribution.

---

## 2) Components of a Bayesian Decision Problem

| Component | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Parameter** $\theta$ | Unknown state of nature |
| **Data** $x$ | Observation from $p(x \mid \theta)$ |
| **Decision rule** $\delta(x)$ | Mapping from data to an action |
| **Loss function** $L(\theta,\delta(x))$ | Penalty for decision $\delta(x)$ when $\theta$ is true |

---

## 3) Loss Functions

| Loss Type | Definition | Typical Use |
|---|---|---|
| **0–1 Loss** | $L(\theta,\delta)=\mathbf{1}\{\theta\neq\delta\}$ | Classification, hypothesis testing |
| **Squared Error Loss** | $L(\theta,\delta)=(\theta-\delta)^2$ | Parameter estimation |
| **Asymmetric Loss** | Higher penalty for one type of error | Medical / safety decisions |

**Example (Umbrella problem):**  
Decision = take umbrella or not.  
Loss reflects being wet vs inconvenience of carrying it.

---

## 4) Frequentist Risk

The **frequentist risk** is the expected loss over repeated samples when $\theta$ is fixed:

$$
R(\theta,\delta)
=
\mathbb{E}_\theta\!\left[L(\theta,\delta(X))\right]
=
\int L(\theta,\delta(x))\,f(x \mid \theta)\,dx
$$

Interpretation:
- Measures long-run performance of a decision rule
- $\theta$ is fixed but unknown

---

## 5) Posterior Risk (Bayesian Perspective)

Given observed data $x$, the **posterior expected loss** is:

$$
\rho(\delta(x))
=
\int_\Theta
L(\theta,\delta(x))\,\pi(\theta \mid x)\,d\theta
$$

### Bayes Action (Optimal Decision)

$$
\delta^\*(x)
=
\arg\min_{\delta(x)} \rho(\delta(x))
$$

This is the **Bayes decision rule**.

---

## 6) Bayes Estimator

> Under **squared error loss**, the Bayes estimator is the **posterior mean**.

### Proof Sketch

Minimize posterior risk:

$$
\rho(\delta(x))
=
\int (\theta-\delta(x))^2\,\pi(\theta \mid x)\,d\theta
$$

Differentiate with respect to $\delta(x)$ and set to zero:

$$
\delta^\*(x)
=
\mathbb{E}[\theta \mid x]
$$

✔ The posterior mean minimizes expected squared error.

---

## 7) Bayes Risk

The **Bayes risk** is the overall expected loss, averaged over both $\theta$ and $x$:

$$
r(\pi,\delta)
=
\iint
L(\theta,\delta(x))\,f(x \mid \theta)\,\pi(\theta)\,dx\,d\theta
$$

Equivalently,

$$
r(\pi,\delta)
=
\int
\rho(\delta(x))\,\pi(x)\,dx
$$

Key points:
- Evaluates a decision rule globally
- The **Bayes rule** $\delta_B$ minimizes $r(\pi,\delta)$

---

## 8) Minimax Decision Rule (Frequentist)

When no prior is assumed, a conservative alternative is the **minimax rule**:

$$
\delta^\*_{\mathrm{minimax}}
=
\arg\min_\delta \max_\theta R(\theta,\delta)
$$

- Protects against the **worst-case** $\theta$
- Purely frequentist (no prior information)

---

## 9) Example — Umbrella Decision

Loss table:

| Nature / Decision | Take Umbrella (T) | Don’t Take (D) |
|---|---|---|
| **Rain (R)** | $0$ (dry) | $10$ (wet) |
| **No Rain (N)** | $1$ (inconvenience) | $0$ |

Let $P(R)=p$.

- Bayes risk of **Take**:  
  $$
  r(T)=1-p
  $$

- Bayes risk of **Don’t Take**:  
  $$
  r(D)=10p
  $$

Decision rule:

$$
1-p < 10p
\;\Rightarrow\;
p > \frac{1}{11} \approx 0.09
$$

**Conclusion:**
- If $P(\mathrm{rain})>0.09$ → take umbrella  
- Otherwise → leave it

---

## 10) Summary

- Decisions combine **data, prior, and loss function**
- Optimal decisions minimize **posterior risk**
- **Posterior mean** is optimal under squared error loss
- **Bayes risk** evaluates rules globally
- **Minimax rule** guards against worst-case scenarios
- Bayesian decision theory connects inference directly to action
