## 1) Bayesian Foundations

### 1.1 Concept

**Bayesian Statistical Inference** is a framework that uses **Bayes’ Rule**  
to combine **prior knowledge** with **observed data**.

**Core idea**  
→ Treat unknown parameters as **random variables** described by probability distributions,  
not as fixed but unknown constants.

**Focus**
- Uncertainty quantification
- Knowledge updating
- Probabilistic reasoning

---

## 1.2 Motivation

Bayesian analysis is attractive because it:

- **Integrates prior knowledge**  
  Encodes existing beliefs or domain expertise.

- **Updates beliefs logically**  
  Beliefs evolve as new data arrive.

- **Provides transparency**  
  Assumptions are explicit and testable.

- **Produces probabilistic conclusions**  
  Enables direct probability statements about parameters.

---

## 2) Bayes’ Rule (Core Equation)

$$
p(\theta \mid x)
=
\frac{p(x \mid \theta)\,p(\theta)}{p(x)}
\propto
p(x \mid \theta)\,p(\theta)
$$

### Interpretation

- **Prior** \(p(\theta)\)  
  → belief before observing data

- **Likelihood** \(p(x \mid \theta)\)  
  → information provided by data

- **Posterior** \(p(\theta \mid x)\)  
  → updated belief after observing data

- **Evidence** \(p(x)\)  
  → normalizing constant ensuring total probability equals 1

---

## 2.1 Key Distributions

| Term | Meaning |
|-----|--------|
| **Prior** \(p(\theta)\) | Belief about parameter before data |
| **Likelihood** \(p(x \mid \theta)\) | Data-generating model |
| **Posterior** \(p(\theta \mid x)\) | Updated belief |
| **Evidence** \(p(x)\) | Model comparison term |
| **Predictive** \(p(x_{\mathrm{new}} \mid x)\) | Prediction with uncertainty |

> Note: In tables, keep math simple (inline only).  
> Use \(x_{\mathrm{new}}\) instead of \(x_{\text{new}}\) to avoid `\text{}` issues.

---

## 3) Bayesian vs. Frequentist View

| Aspect | Bayesian | Frequentist |
|------|---------|-------------|
| Parameter | Random variable | Fixed but unknown |
| Data | Observed once | Repeated samples |
| Output | Posterior distribution | Point estimate + CI |
| Probability | About parameters | About data |

**Key distinction**  
→ Bayesian probability quantifies **belief**,  
→ Frequentist probability quantifies **long-run frequency**.

## 4) “Bayesian” in Machine Learning & CS

### 4.1 Naïve Bayes Classifier

Used in:
- Spam filtering
- Text classification

$$
P(\mathrm{spam} \mid \mathrm{email})
\propto
P(\mathrm{email} \mid \mathrm{spam})\,P(\mathrm{spam})
$$

**Assumption**
- Words/features are conditionally independent given the class.

**Strength**
- Fast
- Interpretable
- Strong baseline model

---

### 4.2 Bayesian Networks

- Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs)
- Represent conditional dependencies
- Useful for:
  - Causal reasoning
  - Probabilistic inference

---

### 4.3 Bayesian Optimization

- Optimizes expensive black-box functions
- Used for:
  - Hyperparameter tuning
  - A/B testing
  - Robotics and control

---

### 4.4 Bayesian Experimental Design

- Adaptive experiment planning
- Minimizes:
  - Sample size
  - Cost
- Example:
  - Early stopping in clinical trials

## 5) Why Bayesian Methods Resurged

- **Computational advances**  
  GPUs + MCMC make inference feasible.

- **Cultural shift**  
  Acceptance of subjective priors.

- **Educational change**  
  Bayesian reasoning now common in modern statistics courses.

---

## 6) Bayesian Workflow

1. **Specify prior**: choose \(p(\theta)\)  
2. **Select likelihood**: define \(p(x \mid \theta)\)  
3. **Compute posterior**: \(p(\theta \mid x)\propto p(x \mid \theta)p(\theta)\)  
4. **Summarize inference**: posterior mean/MAP/credible intervals  
5. **Make decisions**: minimize expected loss under the posterior  

---

## 7) Naïve Bayes: Spam Example (Recap)

$$
P(\mathrm{spam} \mid \mathrm{email})
\propto
P(\mathrm{email} \mid \mathrm{spam})\,P(\mathrm{spam})
$$

- Simple conditional independence assumption
- Often surprisingly effective as a baseline

---

## Key Takeaway

> Bayesian inference formalizes learning from data by updating prior beliefs with evidence.  
> It supports rational probabilistic reasoning under uncertainty and is foundational to modern ML and decision-making.
