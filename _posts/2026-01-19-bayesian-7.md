## 2) Bayesian Hypothesis Testing

Consider two competing hypotheses:

- $H_0: \theta \in \Theta_0$  
- $H_1: \theta \in \Theta_1$

Compute posterior probabilities:

$$
\alpha_0 = P(\Theta_0 \mid x),
\qquad
\alpha_1 = P(\Theta_1 \mid x)
$$

### Decision Rule

- If $\alpha_0 > \alpha_1$: favor $H_0$  
- If $\alpha_1 > \alpha_0$: favor $H_1$  
- If $\alpha_0 \approx \alpha_1$: evidence is inconclusive → revise prior or collect more data  

---

## 3) Bayes Factor

The **Bayes Factor (BF)** measures how much the data support $H_0$ over $H_1$:

$$
B
=
\frac{\text{posterior odds}}{\text{prior odds}}
=
\frac{\alpha_0 / \alpha_1}{\pi_0 / \pi_1}
=
\frac{\alpha_0 \pi_1}{\alpha_1 \pi_0}
$$

For **simple hypotheses** ($\theta=\theta_0$ vs $\theta=\theta_1$):

$$
B
=
\frac{f(x \mid \theta_0)}{f(x \mid \theta_1)}
$$

Interpretation:

- $B>1$: data favor $H_0$  
- $B<1$: data favor $H_1$  

---

## 4) Jeffreys’ Scale for Bayes Factors

| $\log_{10}(B)$ | Bayes Factor $B$ | Evidence Strength |
|---|---|---|
| $0$–$0.5$ | $1$–$3.2$ | Bare mention |
| $0.5$–$1$ | $3.2$–$10$ | Substantial |
| $1$–$2$ | $10$–$100$ | Strong |
| $>2$ | $>100$ | Decisive |

> Jeffreys’ scale provides a qualitative interpretation of numerical Bayes factors.

---

## 5) Bayes Factor vs Posterior Odds

| Quantity | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Bayes Factor** | Evidence from data alone: $f(x \mid H_0)/f(x \mid H_1)$ |
| **Posterior Odds** | Data + prior belief: $\alpha_0/\alpha_1$ |
| **Conflict Case** | BF favors $H_1$ but posterior odds favor $H_0$ → strong prior influence |

## 6) Defective Rate Testing Example

A factory produces items with unknown defect rate $\theta$.  
Products are acceptable if $\theta \le 0.05$.

- Sample size: $n=100$  
- Observed defectives: $x$  
- Prior: $\theta \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(0,1)$  

Posterior:

$$
\theta \mid x
\sim
\mathrm{Beta}(x+1,\;101-x)
$$

Hypotheses:

$$
H_0:\theta \le 0.05,
\qquad
H_1:\theta > 0.05
$$

Posterior probabilities:

$$
\alpha_0(x)
=
\int_0^{0.05}
\mathrm{Beta}(\theta \mid x+1,\;101-x)\,d\theta
$$

$$
\alpha_1(x)=1-\alpha_0(x)
$$

Decision rule:

$$
\frac{\alpha_0(x)}{\alpha_1(x)} > 1
\;\Rightarrow\;
\text{Accept } H_0 \;(\text{qualified})
$$

## 8) Point Null Hypothesis

Testing:

$$
H_0:\theta=\theta_0
\qquad \text{vs} \qquad
H_1:\theta=\theta_1
$$

Posterior probabilities:

$$
\alpha_0(x)
=
\frac{f(x \mid \theta_0)\pi(\theta_0)}{m(x)},
\qquad
\alpha_1(x)
=
\frac{f(x \mid \theta_1)\pi(\theta_1)}{m(x)}
$$

Posterior odds:

$$
\frac{\alpha_0}{\alpha_1}
=
\frac{\pi_0 f(x \mid \theta_0)}
     {\pi_1 f(x \mid \theta_1)}
$$

Bayes factor:

$$
B
=
\frac{f(x \mid \theta_0)}{f(x \mid \theta_1)}
$$

## 9) Coin with Three Possible Biases

A coin is known to be one of:

- Two-headed: $\theta=1$  
- Fair coin: $\theta=0.5$  
- Two-tailed: $\theta=0$  

Prior probabilities:

$$
\pi(0)=0.01,
\qquad
\pi(0.5)=0.98,
\qquad
\pi(1)=0.01
$$

Observed: **4 heads in 4 flips**

Likelihoods:

| Hypothesis | $\theta$ | $P(4\text{ heads}\mid\theta)$ |
|---|---|---|
| $H_1$ | $1$ | $1$ |
| $H_2$ | $0.5$ | $(1/2)^4=1/16$ |
| $H_3$ | $0$ | $0$ |

Apply Bayes’ rule to compute posterior probabilities for each hypothesis.

## 10) IQ Test Example

Assume:

$$
Y \mid \theta \sim \mathcal N(\theta,\,10^2),
\qquad
\theta \sim \mathcal N(100,\,15^2)
$$

Observed score: $y=115$

Posterior:

$$
\theta \mid y
\sim
\mathcal N(110.39,\;63.23)
$$

Test:

$$
H_0:\theta \le 100
\qquad \text{vs} \qquad
H_1:\theta > 100
$$

Posterior probabilities:

$$
P(\theta \le 100 \mid y)=0.106,
\qquad
P(\theta > 100 \mid y)=0.894
$$

Posterior odds $=0.1185$ → strong evidence for $H_1$.

## 11) Handling Point Null in Continuous Models

Problem:  
Under a continuous prior, $P(\theta=\theta_0)=0$.

### Solutions

**(a) Spike-and-slab prior**

$$
\pi(\theta)
=
\pi_0\,\delta_{\theta_0}(\theta)
+
\pi_1\,g_1(\theta)
$$

**(b) Interval null hypothesis**

$$
H_0:\theta \in [\theta_0-\epsilon,\;\theta_0+\epsilon]
$$

## Key Takeaway

> Bayesian hypothesis testing quantifies evidence using posterior probabilities and Bayes factors, offering a coherent alternative to p-values—especially when prior knowledge matters.
