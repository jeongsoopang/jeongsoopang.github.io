## 1) Key Concepts

This section deepens Bayesian inference by covering:

- Difference between **confidence intervals** (Frequentist) and **credible intervals** (Bayesian)
- HPD (Highest Posterior Density) intervals
- Bayesian credible interval construction
- Posterior predictive distributions
- Bayesian hypothesis testing and **Bayes factors**

---

## 2) Confidence vs Credible Interval

### 2.1 Confidence Interval (Frequentist)

A $100(1-\alpha)\%$ confidence interval $C(X)$ satisfies:

$$
P_\theta\!\big(\theta \in C(X)\big) = 1 - \alpha
$$

Key points:
- Probability refers to **random samples**, not to $\theta$
- The parameter $\theta$ is fixed

**Example (Normal mean, $\sigma$ known):**

$$
\bar{X} \pm z_{\alpha/2}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}
$$

---

### 2.2 Credible Interval (Bayesian)

A $100(1-\alpha)\%$ credible interval $I$ satisfies:

$$
P(\theta \in I \mid X) = 1 - \alpha
$$

Key points:
- Probability is taken with respect to the **posterior distribution**
- Directly interpretable as belief about $\theta$ after observing data

---

## 3) Types of Credible Intervals

| Interval Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Equal-Tailed** | Lower $\alpha/2$ and upper $1-\alpha/2$ posterior quantiles |
| **HPD** | Shortest region $B$ such that $\int_B p(\theta\mid x)\,d\theta = 1-\alpha$ |
| **One-Sided** | $[a,\infty)$ or $(-\infty,b]$ |
| **Two-Sided** | Symmetric around mean or median (if posterior is symmetric) |

---

## 4) HPD Interval Definition

For posterior density $\pi(\theta \mid x)$, the **HPD region** is defined as:

$$
\mathrm{HPD}
=
\{\theta : \pi(\theta \mid x) \ge k\}
$$

where the threshold $k$ is chosen such that:

$$
\int_{\{\pi(\theta \mid x) \ge k\}}
\pi(\theta \mid x)\,d\theta
=
1 - \alpha
$$

Properties:
- If the posterior is **unimodal**, HPD is a single interval
- If **multimodal**, HPD may consist of multiple disjoint regions

## 5) Beta–Binomial Model Example

Given:
- Data: $x=11$ successes out of $n=12$
- Prior: $\theta \sim \mathrm{Beta}(7,3)$

Posterior distribution:

$$
\theta \mid x \sim \mathrm{Beta}(18,4)
$$

### MAP Estimate

$$
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{MAP}}
=
\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha+\beta-2}
=
\frac{17}{20}
=
0.85
$$

### 80% Equal-Tailed Credible Interval

$$
[\theta_{0.1},\;\theta_{0.9}]
=
[0.709,\;0.914]
$$

### 80% HPD Interval

$$
[0.7329,\;0.9313]
$$

## 6) Exponential Model with Gamma Prior

Model:
- $X_i \sim \mathrm{Exponential}(\theta)$ (mean parameter)
- Prior: $\theta \sim \mathrm{Gamma}(r,\lambda)$

Posterior:

$$
\theta \mid x
\sim
\mathrm{Gamma}\!\left(
r+n,\;
\lambda+\sum_{i=1}^n x_i
\right)
$$

### Numerical Example

- $n=13$, $\bar{x}=0.21$
- $r=7$, $\lambda=12$

Posterior:

$$
\theta \mid x \sim \mathrm{Gamma}(20,\;39.3)
$$

A 95% credible interval is given by the **2.5% and 97.5% posterior quantiles**.

## 7) Censored Exponential Data Example

Survival model density:

$$
f(t \mid \theta)
=
\frac{1}{\theta} e^{-t/\theta}
$$

Suppose:
- $r$ failures at times $t_1,\dots,t_r$
- $(n-r)$ units survive until time $t_r$

Total exposure time:

$$
T
=
\sum_{i=1}^{r} t_i
+
(n-r)t_r
$$

Likelihood:

$$
L(\theta \mid t)
\propto
\theta^{-r}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{T}{\theta}\right)
$$

With prior $\theta \sim \mathrm{Inv\text{-}Gamma}(\alpha,\beta)$, the posterior is:

$$
\theta \mid t
\sim
\mathrm{Inv\text{-}Gamma}(\alpha+r,\;\beta+T)
$$

## 8) Bayesian Prediction

The predictive distribution for a future observation $X_{n+1}$ is:

$$
p(x_{n+1} \mid D)
=
\int
p(x_{n+1} \mid \theta)\,
p(\theta \mid D)\,
d\theta
$$

Interpretation:
- A **weighted average** of likelihoods
- Weights reflect posterior uncertainty in $\theta$

---

## 9) Bayesian Hypothesis Testing

Posterior probabilities of hypotheses:

$$
P(H_0 \mid x),
\qquad
P(H_1 \mid x)
$$

Decision guideline:
- If $P(H_0 \mid x) > P(H_1 \mid x)$ → favor $H_0$
- If values are close → evidence is inconclusive

---

### 9.1 Bayes Factor

For hypotheses $H_0:\theta=\theta_0$ and $H_1:\theta=\theta_1$:

$$
B_{01}
=
\frac{f(x \mid \theta_0)}{f(x \mid \theta_1)}
$$

Posterior odds:

$$
\frac{P(H_0 \mid x)}{P(H_1 \mid x)}
=
B_{01}
\cdot
\frac{P(H_0)}{P(H_1)}
$$

Interpretation:
- $B_{01}>1$: data support $H_0$
- $B_{01}<1$: data support $H_1$

---

## Key Takeaway

> Bayesian inference provides interpretable uncertainty through credible intervals, principled prediction via posterior averaging, and coherent hypothesis testing using Bayes factors.
