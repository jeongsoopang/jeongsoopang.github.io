## 1) Recap & Posterior Validity

### Bayes’ Rule

- **Discrete**

$$
P(A \mid B)
=
\frac{P(B \mid A)P(A)}{P(B)}
\propto
P(B \mid A)P(A)
$$

- **Continuous**

$$
p(\theta \mid x)
=
\frac{p(x \mid \theta)p(\theta)}{p(x)}
\propto
p(x \mid \theta)p(\theta),
\qquad
p(x)=\int p(x \mid \theta)p(\theta)\,d\theta
$$

A valid posterior must satisfy:

- $\sum_\theta p(\theta \mid x)=1$ (discrete)
- $\int p(\theta \mid x)\,d\theta=1$ (continuous)

---

### Useful Identities

- **Odds form**

$$
\frac{P(\theta_1 \mid Y)}{P(\theta_2 \mid Y)}
=
\frac{P(\theta_1)P(Y \mid \theta_1)}
     {P(\theta_2)P(Y \mid \theta_2)}
$$

- **Law of Total Expectation**

$$
E[U]=E_V\!\left[E(U \mid V)\right]
$$

- **Law of Total Variance**

$$
\mathrm{Var}(U)
=
E[\mathrm{Var}(U \mid V)]
+
\mathrm{Var}(E[U \mid V])
$$

## 2) What Is a Prior?

A **prior distribution** encodes information about parameters **before** observing data.

Important points:

- Priors are not always “subjective”
- They can be motivated by:
  - Mathematical convenience
  - Symmetry or invariance
  - Decision theory
  - Expert elicitation
  - Previous experiments

---

### Types of Priors

- **Conjugate**
- **Informative / weakly informative**
- **Non-informative / reference**
- **Jeffreys prior**
- **Improper priors**
- **Invariant priors**
- **Nonparametric priors**

---

## 3) Conjugate Priors

A prior $\pi(\theta)$ is **conjugate** to a likelihood $p(x \mid \theta)$ if the posterior

$$
\pi(\theta \mid x)
\propto
p(x \mid \theta)\pi(\theta)
$$

belongs to the **same family** as $\pi(\theta)$.

### Why Conjugacy Matters

- Closed-form posteriors
- Simple hyperparameter updates
- Clear interpretation via sufficient statistics

## 4) Core Conjugate Pairs

---

### 4.1 Normal Mean (Variance Known)

- Likelihood:

$$
X \sim \mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2)
$$

- Prior:

$$
\mu \sim \mathcal N(\nu,\eta^2)
$$

- Posterior:

$$
\mu \mid X
\sim
\mathcal N\!\left(
\frac{\eta^2 X+\sigma^2 \nu}{\eta^2+\sigma^2},
\frac{\eta^2\sigma^2}{\eta^2+\sigma^2}
\right)
$$

**Precision form**

$$
\mu \mid X
\sim
\mathcal N\!\left(
\frac{X/\sigma^2+\nu/\eta^2}{1/\sigma^2+1/\eta^2},
\frac{1}{1/\sigma^2+1/\eta^2}
\right)
$$

**Interpretation**

→ Posterior mean is a **precision-weighted average** of data and prior.

---

### 4.2 Bernoulli / Binomial

- Likelihood:

$$
X_i \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(\theta),
\qquad
Y \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(n,\theta)
$$

- Prior:

$$
\theta \sim \mathrm{Beta}(\alpha,\beta)
$$

- Posterior:

$$
\theta \mid X
\sim
\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha+S,\;\beta+n-S),
\qquad
S=\sum_i X_i
$$

**Interpretation**

→ Prior adds **pseudo-counts**.

---

### 4.3 Geometric

- Likelihood:

$$
X \sim \mathrm{Geom}(\theta)
$$

- Prior:

$$
\theta \sim \mathrm{Beta}(\alpha,\beta)
$$

- Posterior:

$$
\theta \mid X=x
\sim
\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha+1,\;\beta+x-1)
$$

---

### 4.4 Poisson

- Likelihood:

$$
X \sim \mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda)
$$

- Prior:

$$
\lambda \sim \mathrm{Gamma}(\alpha,\beta)
$$

- Posterior:

$$
\lambda \mid X=x
\sim
\mathrm{Gamma}(\alpha+x,\;\beta+1)
$$

## 5) Variance Priors & Joint Conjugacy

---

### 5.1 Inverse-Gamma Prior (Mean Known)

- Likelihood:

$$
X \sim \mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2)
$$

- Prior:

$$
\sigma^2 \sim \mathrm{InvGamma}(\alpha,\beta)
$$

- Posterior:

$$
\sigma^2 \mid X
\sim
\mathrm{InvGamma}\!\left(
\alpha+\tfrac12,\;
\beta+\tfrac12(X-\mu)^2
\right)
$$

---

### 5.2 Normal–Inverse-Gamma (Mean & Variance Unknown)

- Prior:

$$
\mu \mid \sigma^2
\sim
\mathcal N(\mu_0,\sigma^2/\kappa_0),
\qquad
\sigma^2 \sim \mathrm{InvGamma}(\alpha_0,\beta_0)
$$

- Posterior updates:

$$
\kappa_n=\kappa_0+1,
\qquad
\mu_n=\frac{\kappa_0\mu_0+X}{\kappa_0+1}
$$

$$
\alpha_n=\alpha_0+\tfrac12,
\qquad
\beta_n=\beta_0+\tfrac12
\frac{\kappa_0}{\kappa_0+1}(X-\mu_0)^2
$$

---

## 6) Multinomial–Dirichlet

- Likelihood:

$$
X \sim \mathrm{Multinomial}(n;\,p_1,\dots,p_k)
$$

- Prior:

$$
\mathbf p \sim \mathrm{Dirichlet}(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k)
$$

- Posterior:

$$
\mathbf p \mid X
\sim
\mathrm{Dirichlet}(\alpha_1+X_1,\dots,\alpha_k+X_k)
$$

---

## 7) Exponential Family Perspective

Many likelihoods can be written as:

$$
p(x \mid \eta)
=
h(x)\exp\{\eta^\top t(x)-g(\eta)\}
$$

Conjugate prior:

$$
\pi(\eta \mid \tau,\rho)
\propto
\exp\{\tau^\top\eta-\rho g(\eta)\}
$$

Posterior:

$$
\pi(\eta \mid x)
\propto
\exp\{(\tau+t(x))^\top\eta-(\rho+1)g(\eta)\}
$$

**Key insight**

→ Conjugacy arises naturally from exponential-family structure.
